September 2026 Monthly Report

From General-Purpose Assistants to
Operational, Governed AI Systems.

September 2026 was defined by the transition from general-purpose assistants toward operational AI systems with data access, persistent workflows, delegated execution, and stronger security boundaries. Apple rebuilt Siri around personal context, OpenAI and Microsoft tied agents to governed enterprise data, NVIDIA and Anthropic moved AI security below the prompt, and the G20 reached consensus on AI standards.

Frontier Models & Platforms — Personal Context Takes Over

Assistants are judged less on isolated answers and more on the context they can reach, the apps they can act in, and the hardware they run on.

Apple Launches Siri AI: A Personal, Cross-Application Assistant

On September 14, Apple began rolling out Siri AI, an extensively redesigned assistant powered by the next generation of Apple Intelligence. Apple described conversational interaction, personal-context understanding, onscreen awareness, broad world knowledge, and additional cross-application actions.

Siri AI can retrieve relevant information from a user’s messages, email, and photos; reason about onscreen material; obtain current information from the web; and perform actions through supported applications. It was released as an English-language beta across iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, watchOS 27, and visionOS 27, with French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish scheduled for October.

Also Announced: New Apple Intelligence Functions

  • AI-powered organisation and monitoring of Safari pages.
  • Description-based creation of custom Safari extensions.
  • Photorealistic generation in Image Playground.
  • Spatial Reframing, Extend, and an upgraded Clean Up tool in Photos.
  • Visual Intelligence across iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro.
  • Description-based automation creation in Shortcuts.

A Consequential Strategy Shift

Apple said the underlying foundation models were built in collaboration with Google and its Gemini models, while processing is divided between compatible devices and Apple’s Private Cloud Compute infrastructure. Apple is combining externally developed model technology with its own device, privacy, and application-integration architecture.

Siri AI Beta Personal Context Onscreen Awareness Built With Gemini

Private Processing Comes to AI Glasses

On September 23, Meta detailed the extension of Private Processing to AI glasses. The confidential-computing system runs AI workloads in confidential virtual machines designed so that host operating systems, hypervisors, and Meta itself cannot read protected user data. It is intended to support sensitive glasses workloads including streaming transcription, contextual search, and long-term recall.

REQUIREMENTS 1–2

Hardware isolation and fail-closed operation: the system refuses to process data if its protections cannot be established.

REQUIREMENTS 3–4

Public verifiability and non-targetability: independent checks of what runs, and no ability to single out a specific user.

REQUIREMENT 5

Encrypted storage controlled through user-provided keys.

Every confidential virtual-machine image is intended to be registered in an append-only, publicly witnessed transparency ledger. If implemented as described, this could provide a stronger privacy boundary for cloud-assisted wearable AI than conventional server-side processing.

Confidential VMs Transparency Ledger Architecture Disclosure

Regulation & Governance — From Declarations to Standards

Global AI coordination shifts toward standards, workforce capacity, intellectual property, and continuous compliance.

G20 Innovation Ministerial Reaches Consensus on Six Pillars

The G20 Innovation Ministerial concluded in North Carolina on September 2 with consensus across six pillars. Ministers also endorsed the Carolina Principles for Emerging Technologies, encouraging foundational research, commercialisation pathways, and trusted deployment. Additional deliverables included AI Prosperity Objectives and an AI Prosperity Compact focused on technical skills and private-sector partnerships.

The Six Consensus Pillars

  • Pro-innovation policy frameworks.
  • Technology for opportunity and prosperity.
  • Technical-workforce development.
  • Intellectual-property policies for AI.
  • AI for standards and standards for AI.
  • Industrial innovation and supply-chain investment.

Why It Is Notable

Global AI coordination is shifting from broad ethical declarations toward standards, workforce capacity, intellectual property, and supply-chain execution.

G20 Consensus · Sep 2 Carolina Principles AI Prosperity Compact

UK Commission Proposes a Staged Framework for Healthcare AI

On September 10, the UK National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare published recommendations based on engagement with more than 12,000 patients, clinicians, members of the public, healthcare leaders, and technology developers.

Principal Recommendations

  • Staged authorisations comparable to learner-driver licensing.
  • Supervised real-world deployment before fuller authorisation.
  • Continuous lifecycle monitoring rather than one-time approval.
  • Publicly searchable safety and adverse-incident information.
  • Clear notification when AI is used in patient care.
  • Stronger MHRA enforcement powers.
  • Human oversight and proportionate, risk-based regulation.

Status & Insight

The commission’s work is advisory; a separate cross-government response is expected. Its central insight is that adaptive AI medical devices cannot be adequately governed through a one-time pre-market assessment alone.

Staged Authorisation Lifecycle Monitoring Advisory Framework

On September 28, NVIDIA announced the Open Agent Safety Platform, an open software platform and reference system for securing agents from testing through deployment, combining governance and control across software and hardware layers.

The platform includes OpenShell, a policy-governed runtime, and Sentry, which uses NVIDIA DOCA and BlueField-4 hardware to verify agent identities, enforce access policies, and produce attested behavioural telemetry. IBM announced support through Agent Identity, HashiCorp Vault, Identity Protection, IBM Fusion, and Red Hat OpenShift integrations.

OpenShell Runtime Sentry · BlueField-4 IBM Integrations

Phased deployment is planned across Claude Code, Claude Enterprise, the Claude Platform, Amazon Bedrock, Claude on AWS, Google’s Agent Platform, and Microsoft Foundry, with broader availability targeted for later in the autumn.

The approach addresses a tension in regulated deployments: customers want model-level threat detection but may be unable to send sensitive prompts and outputs back to the provider.

AI-Assisted R&D — AI Helps Build AI

Frontier labs report rapidly growing use of supervised agents inside their own research and engineering.

OpenAI Reaches Its “Automated Research Intern” Milestone

On September 6, OpenAI said it had reached its internally defined milestone of an automated research intern: a supervised system able to complete well-defined research tasks that might take a skilled researcher several days. Its longer-term stated objective is an automated AI researcher by March 2028.

DAILY AGENT USE

By mid-August, OpenAI’s median researcher used agents daily, consuming more than $600 per day of inference at API list prices.

AGENT-WORKDAYS

The research organisation used an estimated 3.1 agent-workdays for every eight-hour human workday.

EXPERIMENT THROUGHPUT

August produced the highest number of experiments per active experimenter since tracking began in January 2025.

Automated Research Intern AI Researcher Goal: March 2028 August Measurements

Reputed: Claude Leads About 26% of Anthropic’s Model R&D

Reputed September reporting attributed to Anthropic said Claude was leading approximately 26% of its model research-and-development work and participating in some capacity in about 90%. “Leading” was defined as handling most of a task end-to-end from a high-level prompt while remaining under human supervision. The same reporting said roughly 30,000 agents performed research and engineering work at Anthropic during August.

Claude Participates in Some Capacity (Reported) ~90%
Claude Leads End-to-End Under Supervision (Reported) ~26%
Secondary Reporting 30,000 Agents in August
Handle With Care: These are internally defined, company-specific operational metrics disclosed in September, not standardised cross-company measures and not independently audited productivity studies. The two companies use different metrics, yet both describe human-supervised systems completing increasingly large portions of research tasks. This could compress model-development cycles while complicating evaluation: AI-generated research needs stronger experiment provenance, code review, benchmark isolation, and independent confirmation.

IBM · Sep 21

Skill Erosion & the Judgement Gap

IBM’s global study covered 1,500 CHROs and 8,800 employees. Sixty percent of employees worried AI was eroding their skills, with critical thinking most often named as declining. While 71% of CHROs considered the ability to supervise, validate, and override AI essential, only 29% of employees ranked judgement as important.

It also found that 46% of organisations did not involve the CHRO in AI strategy and 72% made limited or no use of AI within HR. Organisations that classified workflows as human-led, AI-assisted, or AI-executed reported 18% risk reduction and 20% quality improvement: associations reported by IBM’s survey, not proof of causation.

Advantage is shifting from the model alone to the context, permissions, and security around it.

Six Structural Shifts of September

Agents Become Persistent

Amazon’s workflows monitor operations continuously, the OpenAI Data agent reasons over connected business data, and Meta’s organisational agent folds verified expert feedback into an evolving knowledge system. Persistence raises the cost of errors.

Security Moves Below Prompts

NVIDIA (hardware-attested agent controls), Anthropic (customer-controlled data retention), and Meta (confidential execution for personal context) all addressed security at infrastructure level rather than relying solely on model alignment.

AI Helps Build AI

OpenAI and Anthropic both reported rapidly increasing use of agents inside model research and engineering. This could compress development cycles but demands stronger experiment provenance, code review, benchmark isolation, and independent confirmation.

Context Becomes Defensible

Fabric IQ, the Data agent, Seller Assistant, and Meta’s organisational agent all rely on proprietary operational context. Models may become interchangeable for some tasks; permissions, business definitions, historical data, and workflow integrations are harder to replace.

Regulation Becomes Continuous

The UK healthcare recommendations and IBM’s horizon scanning both reject static compliance. An AI system cannot be considered permanently compliant at launch: models, tools, user behaviour, data distributions, and rules can all change afterward.

AI Interfaces Commercialise

OpenAI’s Sponsored Agents and Meta One reveal two monetisation models: conversational advertising and paid AI capability tiers. Sponsorship disclosure, neutrality, transaction safeguards, and records of agent claims will matter.

Zero-Trust Principles for Agents

Production AI security is converging on established zero-trust principles, now applied to autonomous agents:

Verified Identities Least-Privilege Credentials Isolated Execution Attested Software Encrypted Storage Behavioural Telemetry Revocable Authority Auditable Actions
September’s Defining Conclusion: September 2026 was the month in which AI systems acquired data access, persistent workflows, delegated execution, and stronger security boundaries. Competitive advantage is shifting toward systems that provide the right model with the right context while preventing unauthorised access or action; the winners will be judged not on isolated answers, but on completed, governed, and verifiable work.
